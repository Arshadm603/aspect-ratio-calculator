How to Calculate Image Print Size from Pixels

Digital screens measure images in pixels, while printers measure artwork in physical inches or centimeters. DPI (Dots Per Inch) bridges that gap by setting how tightly your pixels are packed onto paper.

To check the sharpest print size your photo can handle without looking blurry or pixelated, divide both your pixel width and height by 300.

Frequently Asked Questions

What DPI is best for high-quality printing?

300 DPI is the standard for sharp flyers, posters, greeting cards, and framed wall art viewed up close. Large banners viewed from across a room can safely use 100 to 150 DPI.

Does changing DPI change my image's pixel dimensions?

No. Adjusting DPI only tells a printer how densely to place existing pixels on paper. Your underlying pixel resolution stays identical unless you resample or crop the file.
